The Suite 102 Curly Hair Care Guide

Everything we tell clients in the chair, written down — how to wash, dry, style, and protect your curls between visits.

Curly hair isn't high-maintenance — it's just different. Straight-hair habits (rough towels, daily shampoo, brushing when dry) are exactly what causes the frizz and breakage most people blame on their curls. Here's what actually works.

Know Your Curl Type

Curls generally fall into a few families: loose waves (2A–2C), springy curls (3A–3C), and tight coils (4A–4C). Most heads have more than one type mixed together — tighter at the crown, looser underneath, for example. That's normal. The products and techniques below work across all curl types; where it matters, we've noted the difference.

Washing: Less Often, More Gently

Curly hair produces less visible oil buildup than straight hair because natural oils have a harder time traveling down the curl's bends. Washing every day strips moisture your curls actually need.

  • Wash 2–3 times a week, not daily. Rinse with water on off-days if your scalp feels itchy.
  • Use a sulfate-free shampoo or a "co-wash" (conditioner-only cleanser). Sulfates are harsh detergents that strip curls dry.
  • Detangle in the shower, with conditioner in and fingers or a wide-tooth comb — never a brush on dry curls.
  • Always follow with conditioner. Curly hair needs more moisture than straight hair, every wash.

Drying Without the Frizz

How you dry matters almost as much as what you wash with.

  • Skip the terrycloth towel. Rough fibers rough up the hair cuticle and cause frizz. Use a microfiber towel or an old cotton t-shirt instead.
  • Scrunch, don't rub. Squeeze water and product upward into the roots rather than wiping down the length.
  • Try "plopping" — wrapping damp curls up in a t-shirt or microfiber towel for 10–15 minutes to set the shape before you style.
  • If you diffuse, use low heat and low speed, and dry until about 80% done before letting curls air-dry the rest of the way.

Styling Products That Actually Work

You don't need a dozen products — you need the right few, applied the right way.

  • Leave-in conditioner on soaking-wet hair, before anything else, for slip and moisture.
  • A curl cream or gel applied in sections, scrunched upward, to define the curl pattern and fight frizz as it dries.
  • Go light on oils if your curls are fine — heavy oils can weigh down looser curl patterns. Denser, coilier hair can usually handle more.
  • Don't touch it while it dries. Playing with curls before they've fully set is the #1 cause of frizz after styling.

Protect Your Curls Overnight

  • Sleep on silk or satin — a pillowcase or bonnet. Cotton pulls moisture out of hair and creates friction that leads to frizz and breakage.
  • Try "pineappling" — gathering curls loosely on top of your head with a soft scrunchie before bed — to preserve the shape overnight.

When to Get a Cut

Curly hair should be cut differently than straight hair — curls shrink as they dry and spring up unevenly, so a cut done on wet, stretched hair often looks wrong once it dries and curls back up. That's the whole idea behind a Deva Cut: it's cut curl-by-curl, dry, so the shape is designed around how your curls actually fall.

Most clients do well with a trim every 8–12 weeks — enough to keep shape and remove split ends without cutting into the length you're growing out.
